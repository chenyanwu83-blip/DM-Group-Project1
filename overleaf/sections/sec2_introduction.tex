\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Background}
Automated credit scoring supports lending decisions in which the two error types
are economically asymmetric: approving an applicant who later defaults destroys
principal, while rejecting a creditworthy applicant only forgoes interest. The
Statlog German Credit data encodes this asymmetry in an official 5:1
misclassification-cost matrix~\cite{uci_credit_g_1994}. On the thyroid
screening side, missing a genuine sick-euthyroid patient is the error that
matters, yet such patients form only 6\% of the sick
subset~\cite{uci_thyroid_1986}. In both cases class imbalance makes the
problem harder: accuracy is dominated by the majority class, and a trivial rule
that always predicts the majority already achieves 0.70 accuracy on credit-g
(0.94 on sick) while catching zero positive cases. Three families of remedies
exist --- synthetic over-sampling~\cite{chawla2002smote}, cost-sensitive
training, and threshold adjustment --- but their relative merits depend on the
model family and the degree of imbalance, and are rarely compared under a single
leakage-safe protocol.

For risk management, the question is which intervention actually minimises the
official misclassification cost rather than a surrogate metric. For methodology,
the question is whether the comparison is reproducible: preprocessing frozen
before the main runs, splits locked across the team, and every imputation,
encoding, scaling and resampling step fitted strictly inside the cross-validation
folds. This project addresses both questions on two deliberately contrasting UCI
datasets~\cite{dua2019uci}, using
scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn},
imbalanced-learn~\cite{lemaitre2017imblearn} and
mlxtend~\cite{raschka2018mlxtend}, with Weka~\cite{frank2016weka}
as the course reference tool.

\subsection{Research Questions and Contributions}
The study is organised around four research questions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1.} Under class imbalance and asymmetric costs, how
  should the four candidate strategies (plain, SMOTE, cost-sensitive, threshold
  moving) and four model families be selected?
  \item \textbf{RQ2.} Under what conditions does accuracy mislead model
  selection, and which metrics should drive decisions instead?
  \item \textbf{RQ3.} How do mixed attributes jointly determine feature
  treatment and model selection --- which features matter, and how should nominal
  attributes be encoded for each model family?
  \item \textbf{RQ4.} Do the learnt models make reliable decisions on new,
  unseen examples, as measured once on the locked hold-out sets?
\end{itemize}

Three contributions follow. First, a paired two-dataset design contrasting five
dimensions at once (scale, imbalance, missingness, attribute composition, domain),
with the official 5:1 cost matrix as a first-class evaluation axis. Second, a
full 4~\times~4 experiment matrix on leakage-safe pipelines with locked
splits and fold-level significance tests using the Nadeau--Bengio
correction~\cite{nadeau2003inference,demsar2006comparison}. Third, a
controlled encoding ablation and an interpretability triangulation of Apriori
rules, decision-tree paths and random-forest feature importances
(Section~\ref{sec:apriori}). Both datasets are publicly available from the
UCI repository with donor and license recorded in
Section~\ref{sec:data}; results are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}.
